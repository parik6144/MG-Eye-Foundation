# Netralayam FAQs

Source page: https://www.netralayam.com/faqs

> Note: The answers below are concise paraphrases of the FAQs published on the source page.

## 1. What Can I Expect on My First Visit?
The first visit includes discussion of your eye and medical history, family history, and the reason for consultation, followed by a comprehensive eye examination including vision testing and tonometry. The complete check-up takes around two hours.

## 2. How Often Should I Go to the Ophthalmologist?
A complete eye examination is generally recommended at least once a year. More frequent examinations may be appropriate for people over 55 or those with diabetes, high blood pressure, previous eye injuries, or a family history of eye disease. Sudden vision changes, vision loss, pain, irritation, or medication-related visual effects warrant prompt consultation.

## 3. When Should an Adult Patient with Cataract Be Operated?
For adults, cataract surgery is generally considered when the cataract begins interfering with daily activities. Childhood cataract is treated as more urgent and should be assessed promptly when significant cataract is diagnosed.

## 4. Is Injection Required for Cataract Surgery?
The treating ophthalmologist decides the appropriate method. The page states that more than 95% of cataract surgeries are currently performed without an injection.

## 5. How Do We Select the Type of Lens for Cataract Surgery?
The doctor may suggest two or three suitable lens choices based on the patient's eye condition. The patient makes the final choice. The page notes that visual outcomes can be similar, while dependence on spectacles can vary according to the selected lens.

## 6. How Do I Know If I Am a Candidate for LASIK Eye Surgery?
LASIK is used to correct common refractive problems. Eligibility generally requires being at least 18, having healthy and stable eyes and vision, and not having conditions that make the procedure unsuitable. Certain medical conditions, pregnancy or nursing, eye disease, and unstable vision may affect eligibility. A detailed examination is required before approval.

## 7. How Do You Treat a Lazy Eye in Children?
Treatment depends on the cause and severity and may include corrective spectacles or contact lenses, patching, vision therapy, and surgery when squint or cataract is responsible.

## 8. Can a Lazy Eye Be Corrected with Glasses?
In some cases, glasses can improve a lazy eye. If vision does not improve after approximately 4–8 weeks of proper spectacle use, additional treatment may be considered.

## 9. How Can I Fix My Lazy Eye at Home?
The page describes vision therapy, often combined with glasses, eye patches, or eye drops. Examples of therapy activities include eye tracking, eye jumps, puzzles, dot-to-dot activities, and coloring exercises.

## 10. How Will I Know That I Am Suffering From Glaucoma?
Glaucoma can damage the optic nerve and cause permanent vision loss. Possible warning signs include eye pain, advanced tunnel vision, patchy blind spots, blurred vision, halos, redness, nausea or vomiting, and severe headache.

## 11. What Tests Are Done to Diagnose Glaucoma?
Tests mentioned include tonometry, a dilated eye examination, pachymetry, perimetry, gonioscopy, and RNFL analysis using OCT.

## 12. Can Glaucoma Be Detected in an Eye Test?
Yes. A routine eye examination can detect glaucoma before noticeable symptoms appear, which supports early diagnosis and treatment.

## 13. What Should Be the Lifestyle Dos and Don'ts for a Glaucoma Patient?
The page recommends healthy food and regular exercise, eye protection during activities such as swimming or sports, slow fluid intake, avoiding smoking and eye rubbing, and avoiding foods high in trans fatty acids.

## 14. I Have Refractive Error. What Should I Opt for: Spectacles, Contact Lens, or LASIK?
All three can correct refractive errors, but suitability depends on age, eye health, and personal preference. Children generally use spectacles; contact lenses may be suitable from around 12–13 years in appropriate cases; LASIK requires specific eligibility criteria. An eye examination helps determine the best option.

## 15. How Often Should a Child Get Their Eyes Checked?
The page recommends a first eye examination around six months of age, another by age three, and yearly examinations for school-aged children without vision problems. Children using glasses or contact lenses may need checks every 6–12 months or as advised.

## 16. What Kind of Spectacles Should I Buy for My Child?
Available lens materials include plastic, polycarbonate, and high-index plastic. Polycarbonate and high-index lenses are highlighted for their lighter weight and impact resistance. Glass lenses are discouraged for children because broken glass can injure the eye. Sports goggles may be useful for children involved in contact sports.

## 17. Does a Child with Astigmatism Need Spectacles?
Astigmatism can be corrected with glasses. Spectacles may not always be necessary when the astigmatism is mild and does not produce significant near- or farsightedness. The page notes that glasses may be optional when uncorrected vision is 20/30 or better.

## 18. How Do I Know If My 2-Year-Old Has Eye Problems?
Possible signs include misaligned eyes, an unusually white or grey pupil, watering, discharge or crusting, light sensitivity, frequent eye rubbing, pain or itching, drooping eyelids, poor focus, persistent redness, and poor visual tracking. Some conditions such as lazy eye may have no obvious symptoms, making routine examinations important.

## 19. What Is Normal Eyesight for a 5-Year-Old?
Vision develops with age. The page states that 20/30 can be normal for a five-year-old and recommends an eye examination when visual acuity is below the expected level.

## 20. How Does Diabetes Affect the Eyes?
Long-term uncontrolled diabetes can damage the retina, causing diabetic retinopathy and potentially reducing vision.

## 21. How Is Diabetic Retinopathy Treated?
Treatment may involve retinal laser treatment or injections, depending on the condition and the treating doctor's assessment. Advanced disease may require surgery.

## 22. How Do We Identify Retinal Detachment?
A sudden reduction in vision or a curtain-like shadow across part of the visual field can be a warning sign. The page advises immediate consultation because early treatment and surgery may be required.

## 23. What Is Age-Related Macular Degeneration?
Age-related macular degeneration affects the central retina, which is responsible for most detailed central vision. Early detection is important, and treatment can include injections into the eye.
