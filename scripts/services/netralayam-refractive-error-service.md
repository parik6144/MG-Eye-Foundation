## Refractive Error

Refractive errors are among the most common eye conditions. They occur when the shape of the eye prevents light from focusing correctly on the retina, resulting in blurred vision.

At Netralayam, our refractive error services are designed to diagnose and manage common refractive problems and provide advanced options for reducing dependence on spectacles or contact lenses when appropriate.

## Types of Refractive Errors

### Myopia

Myopia, or short-sightedness, occurs when distant objects appear blurred while nearby objects can be seen more clearly.

### Hypermetropia

Hypermetropia, or long-sightedness, can make nearby objects difficult to see clearly and may cause eye strain.

### Astigmatism

Astigmatism occurs when the cornea or lens has an irregular curvature, causing blurred or distorted vision at different distances.

### Presbyopia

Presbyopia is an age-related change in the eye's ability to focus on nearby objects. It commonly becomes noticeable with increasing age.

## Refractive Error Treatment

Refractive errors can commonly be corrected with spectacles or contact lenses. For selected patients, refractive surgery or an implantable lens may be considered after a detailed eye examination and suitability assessment.

## Our Refractive Error Services

### LASIK

LASIK (Laser-Assisted in Situ Keratomileusis) is a laser refractive procedure used to correct suitable cases of myopia, hypermetropia, and astigmatism. The procedure reshapes the cornea so that light can focus more accurately on the retina.

### PRK

Photorefractive Keratectomy (PRK) is another laser vision-correction procedure. Unlike LASIK, PRK does not create a corneal flap. It may be considered for patients for whom PRK is more suitable based on corneal thickness, shape, lifestyle, or other clinical factors.

### ICL

Implantable Collamer Lens (ICL) is an implantable lens placed inside the eye to correct refractive errors. It can be an option for selected patients, particularly when laser refractive surgery is not suitable.

## Benefits of Refractive Surgery

Depending on the procedure and individual suitability, refractive surgery may offer:

- Reduced dependence on spectacles or contact lenses
- Improved uncorrected distance vision
- Quick visual rehabilitation for many suitable patients
- Treatment customized according to the patient's refractive error and eye condition

A detailed examination is essential before deciding which procedure is appropriate.

## FAQs

### What is a refractive error?

A refractive error is a vision problem caused when the eye does not focus light correctly on the retina. It can result in blurred or distorted vision.

### What are the most common refractive errors?

The most common refractive errors are myopia, hypermetropia, astigmatism, and presbyopia.

### Can refractive errors be treated?

Yes. Refractive errors can generally be corrected with spectacles or contact lenses. Selected patients may also be suitable for laser refractive surgery or implantable lenses.

### Is LASIK suitable for everyone?

No. LASIK is not suitable for everyone. Suitability depends on factors such as age, refractive stability, corneal thickness and shape, eye health, and other medical considerations. A comprehensive eye examination is required.

### What is the difference between LASIK and PRK?

Both procedures use a laser to reshape the cornea. LASIK creates a corneal flap before reshaping the underlying corneal tissue, whereas PRK removes the surface epithelial layer and does not create a flap.

### What is ICL?

ICL is an implantable lens placed inside the eye to correct refractive errors. It can be considered for selected patients who may not be ideal candidates for corneal laser procedures.

### Can refractive surgery permanently remove glasses?

Refractive surgery can significantly reduce dependence on glasses or contact lenses in suitable patients, but it cannot guarantee that glasses will never be needed. Age-related changes such as presbyopia can also affect near vision later in life.
